# Differentiation and developmental potential

Phase 1C; biological foundations. Numerical examples are synthetic unless explicitly identified as user-provided metadata.

## A. Biological meaning

Differentiation is acquisition of specialized cellular properties. Potency describes possible developmental outputs under stated conditions; self-renewal describes production of descendants retaining stem-cell properties. A stem cell has both self-renewal and differentiation capacity.[^STEM]

| Term | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| Totipotent | Potential including embryonic and extraembryonic lineages needed for a whole conceptus |
| Pluripotent | Broad embryonic/body-lineage potential, not whole-conceptus potential by itself |
| Multipotent | Multiple outputs within a more restricted developmental domain |
| Progenitor / precursor | Earlier cell capable of producing downstream cells; usage varies by tissue |
| Mature | Has developed specialized properties; does not automatically mean nondividing |

Potency is not a synonym for youth, marker expression or division rate.[^STEM][^BLOOD]

## B. Mechanism

Signals and transcription factors alter regulatory programs, changing expression and protein function. Fate commitment refers to restriction of developmental options; lineage restriction describes a narrowed set of possible descendants. Terminal differentiation denotes a specialized endpoint in the physiological context under discussion.[^TF][^BLOOD]

Regulatory change → expression change → protein/function change → differentiation is a useful causal outline. It is not a claim that all changes occur simultaneously. Differentiated cells can exhibit plasticity in some contexts; “terminal” is not a universal assertion that reprogramming is impossible.[^PLASTIC]

## C. Relationship to prior concepts

Heterogeneity may reflect cells at different maturation states. The next topic distinguishes such differences from observed descent. A putative mature state does not retrospectively identify a parent cell.[^LINE]

## D. Experimental observation/measurement

Follow descendants and assess specialized outputs to test developmental potential. Colony formation and lineage studies can reveal progenitor output; a proliferating colony alone does not demonstrate unrestricted potency.[^BLOOD] Testing capacity in an altered environment must be distinguished from the fate normally realized in intact tissue.

## F. Relevance to our project

Embryonic brain comparisons must separate progenitor proliferation from neuronal differentiation. Lymphocyte activation can also accompany differentiation. Future representations may need to preserve both specialized function and developmental potential; their equivalence is not assumed.

## G. Common misconceptions

Stem cell ≠ any dividing cell; potency ≠ number of marker genes; maturation ≠ mandatory cell-cycle exit. Comprehension check: two descendants establish at least two realized outputs in that context, not totipotency or indefinite self-renewal.[^STEM]
