# Count distributions and variance

Count data are nonnegative integers when the matrix records discrete molecules, UMIs or fragments. A Poisson model has mean equal to variance; single-cell data commonly show overdispersion, where variance exceeds the mean because of biological heterogeneity, capture variation and unmodeled technical effects. A negative-binomial model adds a dispersion parameter. These are statistical approximations, not claims that every zero or count was generated by that distribution [S36,S37].

For values (0,1,0,4), the mean is 1.25 and population variance is 2.6875. A mean–variance trend can reflect depth and abundance; variance stabilization seeks a scale where technical depth contributes less. Zero inflation is not automatically required: observed zeros can arise from finite sampling, and claims depend on protocol and model [S15]. In single-cell, spatial and multi-omics data, model choice must follow the measured unit and pairing. We describe distributions and do not fit a model or benchmark one. Evidence: Brennecke et al. [S36], Svensson [S15], Hafemeister & Satija [S37].
